The House

The whole world.

DÚNÍYÀ means “the world”, the whole of it. The house exists to bring people and cultures closer through clothing, beginning from Nigeria and reaching outward.

Worth comes before achievement.

We make clothes for people who know they are made in the image of God. They read, travel and ask questions. They build businesses, raise families and make things. They want what they wear to be part of how they live, chosen with the same care.

We call this a philosophical uniform. Clothing is not decoration. It is part of how a person intentionally inhabits the world, and it should carry meaning without needing a speech to explain it.

Restraint is better than noise. We would rather create than follow a trend.

What we hold to

Six values

01

Intentionality

Life is inhabited on purpose. Every line, pocket and seam is there for a reason.

02

Discernment

Judgment is cultivated, so that our decisions can increasingly be trusted.

03

Human dignity

Worth precedes achievement. Our clothes respect and dignify the person wearing them.

04

Growth

We seek understanding through reading, travel, relationships and encounter.

05

Creation

Human beings are creators. We make rather than merely consume.

06

Responsibility

We take responsibility for what we create and influence, and care for what we are given.

2020
Origin

It began in a season of prayer.

In 2020 the founder saw clothing as a way for people to meet other cultures, carry something of home across great distances, and feel the world grow a little smaller. The business started quietly with minimal menswear and custom tailoring. Later we recognised that the first vision and the work we were building were the same thing.

Worldview

The world belongs to God.

We engage cultures through the people who carry them, while discerning the beliefs and practices those cultures contain.

  1. Humanity is diverse, and fundamentally connected.
  2. Cultural difference is valuable. It does not require uniformity.
  3. Encounter between cultures can bring people closer across distance.
  4. That encounter should be intentional and morally discerning.
  5. Human dignity comes before the preservation of any single cultural practice.
